# ECON 594 Thesis

This thesis investigates whether (and to what degree) mergers and acquisitions between large defense contractors during the 2010s affected the U.S. Government's procurement costs. It does so by utilizing novel administrative data on the costs of high-profile, multi-year Department of Defense (DoD) acquisition programs from 2010 to 2023. The empirical strategy combines the differential timing of six, high-profile M&A deals with program-level data on acquisition costs to estimate the effect of contractor consolidation on the costs of acquisition programs in which merging contractors played a key role. I show that contractor consolidation did not significantly affect the DoD's procurement costs during this period. 
After showing that my results are robust to a number of subsample choices, I discuss some potential explanations, and argue that the DoD's buyer power as well the incentives inherent to the market structure of this industry were able to keep a lid on post-merger procurement costs.

This repository contains all code and data used for this paper as well as relevant appendices and supplementary material. Details on dataset construction are available in `data_appendix.md`. This file details the sources which my dataset was compiled from, highlights variables of interest, and describes any assumptions made during compilation as well as how discrepancies were resolved in collecting information from hundreds of documents. The main text of my thesis itself can be found in `matt_econ_594_paper.pdf`.
